## Story 1 — USD 8M Programme Budget Governance & C-Suite Data Storytelling (HSBC)

**Source:** Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation Limited (HSBC) | Strategy PMO | 01/2021 – 09/2022 | Guangzhou, China

**Archetypes:** `group-da` `group-pmo` `group-pdm`

**Situation:**
At HSBC's Platform Strategy Department, I worked as PMO on a USD 8M digital transformation programme spanning 6 workstreams — including Product, Engineering, Marketing, and direct C-suite involvement. The programme lacked a consistent financial tracking mechanism; the C-suite had limited visibility into spend attribution and burn-rate trends across workstreams, creating a gap between execution data and executive decision-making.

**Task:**
I was responsible for owning financial performance analytics across the full programme: building the tracking infrastructure, producing regular reporting, and ensuring decision-makers had the data they needed to steer investment and prioritisation across all 6 workstreams.

**Action:**
I built multi-dimensional budget variance dashboards that tracked spend attribution across all 6 workstreams and produced weekly burn-rate reports escalated directly to C-suite. I served as the central operational interface between Product, Engineering, Marketing, and C-suite — translating complex programme status data into structured insight narratives that drove investment and prioritisation decisions at executive reviews. I also managed risk escalation and coordinated prioritisation decisions across the workstreams without holding direct authority over any of the teams.

**Result:**
The dashboards and weekly burn-rate reports became the standard mechanism for executive decision-making on the programme. Budget risks were surfaced early, enabling timely prioritisation decisions. C-level stakeholders used the reporting outputs to drive investment and resource allocation choices across the full USD 8M programme scope.

**Reflection:**
This role taught me that analytical rigour alone is not enough — the real challenge is translating numbers into narratives that move people to act. I learned to calibrate the level of detail for different audiences and to frame data as a decision-support tool rather than a reporting obligation. If I were to do it again, I would have established the dashboard framework earlier in the programme lifecycle to capture baseline data from the very start.
